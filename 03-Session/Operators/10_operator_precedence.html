Operator Precedence

When multiple operators are present in an expression, operator precedence determines the order of evaluation. Understanding precedence is crucial for writing correct Python code.

3 + 10 * 2
= 23
(3 + 10) * 2
= 26

Operator Precedence (Highest to Lowest)

  1. ()
    Parentheses
  2. **
    Exponential operator
  3. ~, -
    Bitwise complement, unary minus
  4. *, /, %, //
    Multiplication, division, modulo, floor division
  5. +, -
    Addition, subtraction
  6. <<,>>
    Left and right shift
  7. &
    Bitwise AND
  8. ^
    Bitwise XOR
  9. |
    Bitwise OR
  10. >, >=, <, <=,==, !=
    Relational / comparison operators
  11. =, +=, -=, *=, ...
    Assignment operators
  12. is, is not
    Identity operators
  13. in, not in
    Membership operators
  14. not
    Logical NOT
  15. and
    Logical AND
  16. or
    Logical OR

Worked Examples

Example 1: Basic Precedence
a = 30
b = 20
c = 10
d = 5

print((a + b) * c / d) → 100.0
print((a + b) * (c / d)) → 100.0
print(a + (b * c) / d) → 70.0
Example 2: Complex Expression
Expression: 3/2*4 + 3 + (10/5)**3 - 2

Step 1: 3 / 2 * 4 + 3 + (10 / 5) ** 3 - 2
Step 2: 3 / 2 * 4 + 3 + 2.0 ** 3 - 2
Step 3: 3 / 2 * 4 + 3 + 8.0 - 2
Step 4: 1.5 * 4 + 3 + 8.0 - 2
Step 5: 6.0 + 3 + 8.0 - 2
Step 6: 15.0

Result: 15.0
